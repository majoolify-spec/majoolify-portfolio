## Overview

This build focused on a problem that appears in many modern product teams: UI review is fast in theory, but slow in practice because screenshots, test state, commentary, and next actions live in different places.

The product surface was designed to make that loop tighter. Instead of treating testing as a purely technical layer, the interface framed review as an editorial workflow with capture, comparison, and action signals living close together.

## What I handled

- Product-facing frontend architecture with a route structure that could support capture, project history, and settings without collapsing into tool clutter.
- UI refinement aimed at making dense operational actions feel calm and legible.
- AI-oriented framing so prompt-assisted review features could fit the product without reading like bolted-on demos.

## Why it mattered

The key value was not only the feature set. It was the reduction of friction between people reviewing the interface and people moving the product forward. That required the design language, information hierarchy, and implementation details to pull in the same direction.

## Delivery notes

The resulting product direction emphasized:

- a strong operational shell,
- fast scanning of states and outputs,
- and a more intentional visual system than the average internal tool.

The project is representative of the work I like most: product logic, frontend execution, and AI-aware workflow design in the same system.
